# Supplied-board profiling baseline

The later [high-resolution profiling report](image-performance-results.md) supersedes this preliminary source set. The results below remain historical evidence for the original small-image board.

This is a preliminary measurement for issue #8, not a completed performance validation or a production tuning decision. The supplied board has 30 real JPEGs, but no high-resolution photos. The longest source edges range from 600 to 1594 pixels. The source files total 3,505,118 bytes (3.34 MiB). SPEC.md also requires several high-resolution photos, so the current set does not cover the complete workload.

## Device and build

The physical device is a [Samsung Galaxy Tab S9 FE+ 5G](https://www.samsung.com/ph/support/model/SM-X616BLIAXTC/) (SM-X616B), running Android 16. Its physical display is 1600 × 2560 pixels at 340 dpi. Android reports a 256 MiB app memory class, giving the current memory-class/8 cache a 32 MiB budget. Measurements use the debug build of `532d579` with this continuation's counter-snapshot and profiling harness changes. APK SHA-256 values are retained with the measurement metadata. Kernel-reported usable RAM is 7,613,916 KiB (7.26 GiB). The contributor did not attach a debugger or Android Studio profiler. Developer options, debug logs, and instrumentation were enabled. The reported thermal status remained 0 before and after the full-screen capture; this does not rule out every form of throttling.

## Workload and measurement limits

The preliminary full-screen run uses three 30-second pan rounds, three 30-second zoom rounds, and three real-source batch imports. Each batch imports three temporary copies through the normal importer, briefly increasing the board from 30 to 33 items, then removes only those new items. A separate renderer experiment compares 16, 32, and 64 MiB cache budgets, with three 30-second rounds per budget. It does not draw frames and cannot establish UI frame performance. Floating-window measurements are captured separately after the lead enabled overlay permission.

The current run is shorter than the documented two-minute workloads. Repetitions share one process; counters are measured as stage deltas. Programmatic viewport updates bypass canvas gesture recognition; the lead's manual gesture and visual-quality checks remain unverified. Memory and `gfxinfo` are polled every 0.5 seconds. Reported PSS peaks are the largest observed samples. Frame counts omit flagged or incomplete rows and may omit frames outside the bounded polling history. Durations above 16.67 ms use a fixed 60 Hz threshold even though the device's captured frame interval is approximately 11.11 ms (90 Hz). Frozen frames use a 700 ms threshold. Percentiles use linear interpolation between ordered samples. Decode times exclude queue time and canceled jobs. Refresh cache hit ratios are frequency-weighted observations, not unique image requests.

The tablet's epoch clock differs from the computer's, so the capture script measures and applies the clock offset. Device monotonic timestamps directly align frame rows with workload markers. Per-run clock uncertainty is retained in the capture metadata.

## Results

The full-screen run captured 11,078 usable frames across pan, zoom, and import stages. There were 58 durations above 16.67 ms (0.52%) and none above 700 ms. The worst captured duration was 26.00 ms. All three import batches succeeded. No out-of-memory error or decode failure appeared in the captured logs. The test passed its persisted-snapshot equality check after restoring the original 30 items and both viewports.

Pan rounds had largest sampled PSS values of 196.1, 193.0, and 196.2 MiB. Their frame p95 durations were approximately 11.66, 11.81, and 11.82 ms. Completed decode counts were 53, 19, and 19. Refresh-hit ratios were 99.61%, 99.94%, and 99.95%.

Zoom rounds had largest sampled PSS values of 281.1, 275.7, and 277.3 MiB. Their frame p95 durations were approximately 10.94, 11.69, and 11.80 ms. Completed decode counts were 992, 987, and 988. Median completed decode time was 8 ms in each round; decode p95 values were 18, 20, and 20 ms, and the maximum observed decode was 50 ms. Refresh-hit ratios were 77.55%, 77.87%, and 78.65%. Completed sample factors ranged from 1 to 8. These repeated decodes occurred during deliberately repeated overview/detail changes; they are not proof of redundant decoding at a fixed viewport.

The renderer-only 16 MiB experiment completed 566, 576, and 556 decodes. The 32 MiB experiment completed 553, 557, and 564. The 64 MiB experiment completed 526, 552, and 543. Median completed decode times ranged from 9 to 10 ms, and per-round p95 values ranged from 27 to 32 ms. Maximum observed cache occupancies were 12.16, 19.88, and 30.65 MiB respectively. Larger budgets produced more sample-factor-1 decodes, while the 16 MiB budget produced more sample-factor-4 decodes. Cache refresh-hit ratios decreased from approximately 90% at 16 MiB to 86% at 32 MiB and 85% at 64 MiB; sharper decodes may take longer and cause additional refresh misses while in flight; these captures do not isolate that contribution. This ratio alone does not establish that the smaller cache is better.

The full-screen capture's clock offset was -44.5870 seconds, with estimated round-trip uncertainty of 17.1 ms. Overlay permission was enabled through Settings during this invocation; the experiment is preliminary and is not a controlled comparison of production builds. The independent floating capture follows below.

[The anonymized full-screen summary](profiling/2026-10-02/full-screen-summary.json) contains each stage's peak-memory breakdown, frame durations, decode statistics, sample-factor distribution, and cache counter deltas. [The source inventory](profiling/2026-10-02/source-inventory.json) records dimensions, format, and encoded size without filenames or asset IDs. Raw captures remain local in `/tmp/easlie-issue8-baseline`; the earlier harness check is in `/tmp/easlie-issue8-validation-fullscreen`.

The independent floating-range invocation passed three 30-second rounds. It alternated the actual floating canvas between 561 × 195 and 2475 × 1117 pixels, confirming that touch injection changed the real window size. Each round returned to the activity host. The measured activity-host canvas in this invocation was 2458 × 722 pixels; this is the app's available canvas, not the physical display size.

Floating stages captured 5,324 usable frames, with 1,652 durations above 16.67 ms (31.03%) and 0 above 700 ms. Per-round frame p95 durations were 28.38, 29.88, 27.82 ms, and the maximum captured duration was 66.16 ms. Largest sampled PSS values were 230.71, 251.76, 249.87 MiB. Completed decode counts were 979, 971, 948, with p95 times of 36.00, 34.50, 33.00 ms. Refresh-hit ratios were 71.09, 71.93, 70.33%. No out-of-memory error or decode failure appeared in the captured logs. The test passed the persisted-snapshot restoration check. Reported thermal status was 0 before and after capture. After profiling, an external backup comparison confirmed the original manifest data, byte-for-byte equality of all 30 original assets, and no extra board files. Android reported no easlie service or window before the activity was relaunched with the restored board.

[The anonymized floating summary](profiling/2026-10-02/floating-summary.json) contains per-stage memory breakdowns and counter deltas. Raw captures remain local in `/tmp/easlie-issue8-floating-range`. The earlier limited-range run is retained locally in `/tmp/easlie-issue8-floating` as harness-validation evidence. Floating stages include attach, resize, viewport changes, teardown, and return, so their frame totals do not isolate resize cost from transitions. The higher slow-frame proportion is a measured reason to investigate floating-window relayout and bitmap uploads with a system trace; it does not identify a cause by itself.

## Policy status and remaining work

The production cache budget, visible/nearby allocation split, power-of-two resolution tiers, and hysteresis thresholds remain provisional. No new value is selected from this limited source set. A larger cache's memory cost and image clarity must be assessed using the required high-resolution sources before choosing a policy.

The required follow-up is a representative 20-to-30-image board containing several original-resolution photos, sustained repeated workloads, independent-process comparisons, and the lead's visual/gesture smoke test. A suitable follow-up replaces several current items with high-resolution photos rather than expanding the baseline beyond 30 items. The issue remains open until the chosen production policy and its measured support are documented.
